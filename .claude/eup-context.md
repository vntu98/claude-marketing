## Product Overview

**One-liner:** eUp Technology Joint Stock Company is an EdTech company that develops and operates foreign-language learning applications, dictionaries, and exam-prep products.

**What it does:** Founded in 2016, eUp builds and ships a portfolio of mobile-first educational products focused mainly on Japanese, Chinese, English, Korean, and German learning. The ecosystem combines dictionary utilities, structured learning, test preparation, and learning-through-content products. AI, smart data, and personalization are used to guide learning paths and improve long-term progress.

**Product category:** B2C EdTech / language-learning / exam-prep

**Product type:** Mobile-first learning product ecosystem with companion web, affiliate, and content surfaces

**Business model:** Freemium with premium upgrades, subscriptions, affiliate distribution, and selected education or platform partnerships

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** Language learners often use fragmented resources with no clear next step, which makes progress inconsistent and exam prep inefficient.

**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Generic language apps feel shallow for serious learners or exam candidates
- Books, YouTube, and PDFs provide content but not personalized sequencing or feedback
- Dictionary tools help in the moment but do not automatically build habit or retention

**What it costs them:** Lost study momentum, inconsistent results, weaker exam confidence, and extra time spent deciding what to study next

**Emotional tension:** Learners feel they are trying hard without visible progress, worry they are studying the wrong material, and lose confidence after gaps in consistency

**Direct:** news-based language apps, bilingual subtitle/video learning products, content-led study products

## Differentiation

These are portfolio-level differentiators. Do not turn them into app-specific claims unless supported by task-specific evidence.

**Key differentiators:**
- Portfolio depth across multiple learner intents: lookup, structured study, exam prep, and real-world content
- Stronger fit for Asian-language learning and exam scenarios where generic gamified apps are often too broad
- AI, smart data, and personalization used to guide next actions instead of leaving learners with content overload
- Clearer bridge from acquisition content to activation, habit formation, and premium upgrade moments

**How we do it differently:** Connect dictionary utilities, guided practice, exam prep, content-based learning, and monetization surfaces into one learner journey rather than treating each touchpoint as a disconnected product

**Why that is better:** Learners get faster time-to-value, better retention, and more confidence about what to study next

**Why customers choose us:** They want practical progress, clearer guidance, and products that feel more aligned with real study goals than generic entertainment-first language apps

## Objections

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "I already use free resources." | Free resources help discovery, but learners still need sequencing, review, and accountability to make progress stick. |
| "Language apps feel too gamified and shallow." | Position the portfolio around practical outcomes, exam readiness, and clear learning pathways, not only streak mechanics. |
| "I don't know which app or plan is right for me." | Use segmentation, placement, and onboarding to route learners toward the right path, language, and premium offer. |

**Anti-persona:** Learners looking only for entertainment with no intent to build habit, and teams wanting generic top-of-funnel vanity traffic with no activation or retention strategy

## Switching Dynamics

**Push:** Fragmented resources, unclear progress, weak habit formation, poor exam confidence

**Pull:** Personalized learning, clear next steps, practical content, and smarter review loops

**Habit:** Learners stay with familiar free tools even when progress is shallow because switching feels like resetting

**Anxiety:** Fear of paying for another app that gets abandoned after a week, uncertainty about level placement, and concern that content will not match personal goals

## Goals And KPI Hierarchy

**Business goal:** Grow a durable language-learning ecosystem that converts acquisition into activation, habit formation, premium upgrades, and partner growth while strengthening eUp's position as a leading EdTech company in Asia.

Use this KPI order by default unless the task explicitly overrides it.

### 1. Company North Star

- **Primary default KPI:** activated learners
- Working definition: learners who reach a first meaningful value moment, typically `lesson_completed` after onboarding or level placement

### 2. Acquisition KPI

- **Primary acquisition KPI:** qualified `signup_completed`
- Supporting KPIs: landing page conversion, CTA click-through, channel quality, affiliate-attributed signups

### 3. Activation KPI

- **Primary activation KPI:** `signup_completed` → `placement_test_completed` → `lesson_completed`
- The most important activation checkpoint is not raw signup volume, but conversion into first meaningful study value

### 4. Monetization KPI

- **Primary monetization KPI:** `subscription_started`
- Supporting KPIs: `paywall_viewed`, activated learner to subscription conversion, plan mix

### 5. Retention KPI

- **Primary retention KPI:** retained activated learners
- If a task needs a practical proxy and retention data is not directly available, use repeat lesson completion and return-session behavior as interim evidence

### 6. Partner KPI

- **Primary partner KPI:** affiliate-attributed activated learners
- Supporting KPI: `affiliate_signup_completed`

**Operating rule:** Do not optimize vanity traffic, installs, clicks, or follower growth ahead of activation and monetization quality unless the task explicitly says awareness is the goal.
