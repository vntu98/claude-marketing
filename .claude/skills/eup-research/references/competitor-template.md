# Competitor Research Overlay

Use this template whenever research feeds positioning, GTM strategy, pricing, SEO, social planning, or product requests.

## 1. Market Map

Separate the landscape into:

- **Direct competitors**: same job, same buyer, same shortlist
- **Secondary / adjacent competitors**: partial overlap or different entry point
- **Substitutes**: internal build, spreadsheet/manual process, agency, freelancer, or do-nothing option

## 2. Minimum Evidence Per Competitor

| Field | What to capture |
|------|------------------|
| Type | Direct / secondary / substitute |
| ICP / use case | Who they appear to serve best |
| Positioning | Promise, angle, category language |
| Strengths | What they do clearly well |
| Weaknesses | Product, trust, pricing, UX, or service gaps |
| Pricing / packaging | Tiers, free plan, trial, notable constraints |
| Customer praise | Repeated positive themes with quotes when available |
| Customer complaints | Repeated negative themes with quotes when available |
| Switch triggers | Why buyers move to or away from them |
| SEO / content posture | Topics they own, gaps they leave open |
| Sources | URLs, review pages, dates, notes |

## 3. Required Output File

Save competitor work to `reports/research/YYYYMMDD-[slug]/competitor-landscape.md`.

Suggested structure:

```markdown
# Competitor Landscape

## Market Map

| Category | Competitor | ICP / use case | Why buyers consider them |
|------|---------|----------------|----------------------------|

## Comparison Matrix

| Competitor | Type | Positioning | Strengths | Weaknesses | Pricing / packaging | Review themes | SEO / content posture | Win / loss guidance | Sources |
|---------|------|-------------|-----------|------------|---------------------|---------------|-----------------------|---------------------|-------|

## Key Takeaways
- Which competitors are strongest in each situation
- Message, product, or SEO gaps
- When to compete directly versus when to change the angle

## Our SWOT

### Strengths
- ...

### Weaknesses
- ...

### Opportunities
- ...

### Threats
- ...
```

## 4. Guardrails

- Use exact quotes from reviews or forums when possible.
- Mark inferred statements clearly.
- Do not flatten direct, adjacent, and substitute competitors into one list.
- If pricing is unavailable, state that instead of guessing.
- End with explicit guidance:
  - where we win
  - where they win
  - where the market is crowded
  - where product or dev changes could create leverage
