## Motivation & JTBD Quotes

**Q01** — Career and communication drive
> "Mainly, I study English for my future career. Since my major is multimedia, most of the documents are translated from English."
— Vietnamese university student (S13), academic study on EFL motivation
Source: PMC/NCBI peer-reviewed study (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8093461/)
Signal: Career access is a dominant instrumental motivation; not just fluency for its own sake

**Q02** — International connection
> "I learn English for communicating with international friends... Yes, my motivation is communication... I truly want to talk to them in English."
— Vietnamese university student (S1), same study
Source: PMC/NCBI peer-reviewed study
Signal: Communicative aspiration; the gap between wanting to speak and being unable to is the core JTBD tension

**Q03** — Entertainment as entry point
> "My motivation to learn English was to listen to music and to play the guitar. If I am good at English, I will be able to get access to the international US-UK music."
— Vietnamese university student (S10), same study
Source: PMC/NCBI peer-reviewed study
Signal: Entertainment content (music, film, video) is a genuine pull into English learning, not just a study tool

**Q04** — Intrinsic interest, not parental pressure
> "English is not an important compulsory subject, but I like it. It is not due to my parents, but it is simply my own interest."
— Vietnamese university student (S2), same study
Source: PMC/NCBI peer-reviewed study
Signal: A meaningful sub-segment is intrinsically motivated; these are the power users most likely to sustain habit

**Q05** — Medical English via movie context (VnExpress learner anecdote)
> "I used to watch Grey's Anatomy because I was learning medical English at that time. That was when I learned about 'OR – operating room,' 'complication,' or other medical terms, and I was so excited that I still remember those words even though I'm not using them often now."
— Vietnamese English learner quoted in VnExpress (2024)
Source: VnExpress article "4 bước để thành thạo tiếng Anh qua phim" (https://vnexpress.net/4-buoc-de-thanh-thao-tieng-anh-qua-phim-4742979.html)
Signal: Emotional engagement with content creates durable vocabulary memory; topic-relevant video beats generic drills

## Pain Points & Frustration Quotes

**Q06** — Lack of real-world English exposure
> "The major barrier to English communications is that English is not used much in daily life. The ways that I can be exposed to English are from textbooks, supplementary materials, and the Internet."
— Vietnamese university student (S2)
Source: PMC/NCBI peer-reviewed study
Signal: Video content is one of the few authentic English environments accessible to Vietnamese learners without travel or expensive tutors

**Q07** — Limited contact hours with real English
> "We only work with books, our teacher and friends in class and the Internet at home. One pity is that there are not many opportunities to see and communicate with international friends regularly."
— Vietnamese university student (S9)
Source: PMC/NCBI peer-reviewed study
Signal: Video learning fills an authentic input gap that textbooks and classrooms cannot provide

**Q08** — Heavy workload crowds out language study
> "There may be too many other things to do at home or in class. There are too many subjects with heavy workloads."
— Vietnamese university student (S7)
Source: PMC/NCBI peer-reviewed study
Signal: Passive/entertainment-first formats (watching video) have a lower activation cost than dedicated study sessions

**Q09** — Vocabulary saving failures frustrate active learners
> [Forum topics show repeated reports of:] "Saving words/phrases not working" / "Translation errors (machine and ASR)" / "Extension functionality failures"
— Multiple users on Language Learning with Netflix community forum
Source: Language Learning with Netflix Forum (https://forum.languagelearningwithnetflix.com/)
Signal: Word-save and vocabulary pipeline reliability is a top pain point for dedicated learners; bugs kill retention loops

**Q10** — Bilingual subtitle quality degrades with non-Romance languages
> "I find that Lingopie's functions are more polished on languages like German and Spanish, but still have a long way to go on languages like Japanese and Chinese."
— Reviewer, The Language Closet (2024)
Source: Lingopie Review 2024 (https://thelanguagecloset.com/2024/08/03/lingopie-review-2024/)
Signal: Vietnamese/Asian language support is often an afterthought; subtitle quality and tokenization are real issues for SEA learners

**Q11** — Translation cost friction (Trancy pricing change)
> "The app used to allow auto-generated translations but got rid of that, so users now have to use their AI translation which costs money."
— Trancy user review
Source: Trancy AI Review 2025, Skywork.ai (https://skywork.ai/blog/trancy-ai-review-2025-bilingual-subtitles-language-learning/)
Signal: Learners expect translation/bilingual subtitles to be free or near-free; premium paywalls on core features cause churn

**Q12** — eJOY secondary subtitle translation quality issues
> [Secondary subtitle] "appears to be translated from Google Translate, which sometimes produces confusing translations. When a long sentence is split across multiple display lines, the translation only translates the portion currently visible on screen rather than the complete sentence."
— User on Tinhte.vn (Vietnamese tech forum)
Source: Tinhte.vn forum (https://tinhte.vn/thread/lam-cach-nao-de-cai-dat-phu-de-song-ngu-tren-youtube.3400443/)
Signal: Vietnamese-market learners are sensitive to translation accuracy; partial-sentence translation bugs break the learning experience

**Q13** — Unattractive materials = demotivation
> "Unattractive learning materials and a lack of authentic communicative activities contributed to learning demotivation."
— Research finding, 2024 Vietnamese EFL motivation study (271 students)
Source: Language Learning Motivation Among Vietnamese EFL Students, Academia.edu
Signal: Generic drills and textbook exercises fail to hold attention; authentic video content is the antidote

**Q14** — Passive watching does not equal learning
> [Vietnamese learners warned against:] "Watching without subtitles" and "passive viewing" — the article emphasizes that "effective learning requires active engagement—mimicking dialogue and intentionally practicing pronunciation, not just passive entertainment consumption."
— OEA Vietnam (English learning education blog)
Source: OEA Vietnam article (https://oea-vietnam.com/hoc-tieng-anh-qua-phim-co-de-dang-nhu-ban-nghi/)
Signal: Learners know passive watching isn't enough; they want structured active tools (shadowing, repeat, dictation) layered on content

**Q15** — Migaku interface complexity deters casual users
> "Various aspects of the app [are] confusing with less straightforward design, and juggling the desktop app and browser extension slowed down the learning process and felt like a chore."
— Migaku user review
Source: Migaku Review, Root-Nation.com (https://root-nation.com/en/soft-en/apps-en/en-migaku-app-review/)
Signal: Advanced learner tools suffer from setup friction; mobile-first, one-tap UX is a genuine differentiator

## Desired Outcomes & Vocabulary Quotes

**Q16** — Vocabulary in context sticks longer
> "The subtitles are very useful to learn english, improve listening skills and vocabulary."
— eJOY app user review (Apple App Store, US)
Source: Apple App Store, eJOY Learn English with Videos (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ejoy-learn-english-with-videos/id1258329729)
Signal: Vocabulary improvement and listening skills are the two outcomes learners explicitly name; subtitles are the perceived mechanism

**Q17** — Self-directed English learners want to spend time watching, not building flashcards
> "It is more helpful than any other tool I've used. I had been using podcasts before and trying to make flashcards, and that worked pretty well. But it ended up that I was spending more time making the flashcards than studying. FluentU really solves this."
— FluentU user quote (widely cited in multiple FluentU reviews)
Source: FluentU Language Learning reviews (cited via Lingopie comparison articles)
Signal: Vocabulary-from-video with automatic flashcard creation is a high-value JTBD; the job is "remember what I just watched" without extra effort

**Q18** — Learners want to click any word and get instant help
> "Tap any word in either language to see instant definitions, usage examples, grammar notes, and cultural context without pausing or leaving the video, and save clicked words automatically to your personal flashcard deck with video context for later review."
— Trancy product description (aspirational UX, reflects what learners are being sold)
Source: Trancy.org (https://www.trancy.org/)
Signal: "Tap-to-translate + auto-save" is now the baseline expectation; products that require manual copy-paste feel broken

**Q19** — YouTube gives learner control and autonomy
> "Learners are more in control of their rate of learning as they are able to rewind, pause and refresh their memory at their own will."
— Research finding, Van Lang University YouTube study (333 students)
Source: International Journal of TESOL & Education (https://i-jte.org/index.php/journal/article/view/485)
Signal: Learner control and self-pacing are key perceived benefits of video vs. classroom; app must preserve this feel

**Q20** — Vietnamese learners prefer mobile apps (50%) over other methods
> "50% primarily utilize mobile apps... 33% online lessons... 32% YouTube, 31% books, 29% language centers"
— EdTech Agency Vietnam market analysis
Source: EdTech in Vietnam: The English Learning Segment (https://edtechagency.net/edtech-in-vietnam-the-english-learning-segment/)
Signal: Mobile-first is not optional for Vietnam — half of self-directed learners are app-first; YouTube (32%) is the #3 method

**Q21** — Bilingual subtitle content availability is scarce and valuable
> [ConTuHoc article emphasizes:] Learners want to "tra cứu từ vựng ngay trong lúc xem" (look up vocabulary instantly while watching) — bilingual subtitles are described as essential for "dễ dàng theo dõi và hiểu nội dung" (easily following and understanding content)
— Vietnamese education blog, ConTuHoc.com
Source: ConTuHoc.com TOP 7 app article (https://www.contuhoc.com/app-hoc-tieng-anh-qua-phim-va-video)
Signal: The combination of bilingual subtitles + in-video lookup is the minimum viable feature set Vietnamese learners expect

## Market Context Quotes

**Q22** — English proficiency gap is significant
> "Only 58% reach B1 level; merely 5% achieve B2"
— Vietnam market data, EdTech Agency
Source: EdTech in Vietnam: The English Learning Segment (https://edtechagency.net/edtech-in-vietnam-the-english-learning-segment/)
Signal: Most learners are stuck in lower-intermediate; content and tools must be calibrated to A2-B1 range, not advanced fluency

**Q23** — Comprehension improvement with subtitles is measurable
> "After watching just one episode of a TV show with English subtitles, a group of ESL students showed a 17% improvement in comprehension compared to those who didn't."
— 2016 academic study, cited in Preply article
Source: Preply, Using Subtitles for Language Learning (https://preply.com/en/blog/using-subtitles-for-language-learning/)
Signal: Subtitle-aided video learning has empirical backing; useful for product positioning and trust-building messaging
